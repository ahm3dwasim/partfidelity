# PartFidelity features

Every shipped feature: what it does and why we built it.

Overview, for a first read:

- PartFidelity turns one repair video into a step-by-step guide with a hard stop at dangerous steps.
- Extraction: Muse Spark reads the video three times and a disagreement fails closed.
- Parts: SAM 3.1 boxes the fan and battery; only those two are located.
- Labels: a Muse Spark read-back, then a Spark Open (open weights) second check in the background that only asks a person to review.
- Receipts: every model call leaves a receipt page, and spend is reserved before any call.
- Offline: the edit check behind `/catch` and the home-page film are built and run outside the live request path.
- The architecture map is in the [README](../README.md#how-it-fits-together); the entries below are the full record of what was built, in file order.

## Atomic admission and ledger
- What it does: Admits at most 2 running and 10 waiting jobs per 240-second deadline, and authorizes every paid call through atomic reserve-before-spend: each `JobContext.reserve` holds its worst case against the $0.50 job cap, the $0.46 per-job reservation ($0.26 3-run Spark / $0.08 32-call SAM / $0.12 read-back), row/day/aggregate ceilings, then settles actual cost (an overrun past the reservation or job cap still lands as actual spend on every row, trips the breakers, and raises) and releases unused holds; refusal always raises before client invocation. `finish` is the single active → done/failed transition: `done` atomically keeps exactly the requested provisional set and queues every other provisional for sweeper deletion (due at once, bytes gone only after the tombstone), `failed` keeps nothing and clears every provisional keep; any failure rolls the keeps and the transition back together, and an exact same-set replay is idempotent. `record_call_with_event` persists each model-call receipt and its funnel event in one keyed transaction over the ledger's tables, enforcing the evidence module's exact call/event allowlists: exact replay is a no-op, a conflicting payload raises, and a mid-write failure leaves neither row behind.
- Why we built it: Cost safety — no paid call starts unless its job, budget row, daily breaker, and aggregate cap are atomically reserved, so contention, timeouts, and breaker trips fail closed with zero authorizations instead of spending first and checking later.
## Durable upload lifecycle
- What it does: Owns the SQLite `UploadStore`: preview registration against an admitted job and its live `upload_preview` artifact, single-winner confirmation claims that persist the provider-queryable claim id and the absolute 25-hour guard deadline before any Meta I/O, atomic Meta-row attachment with independent request/now lower bounds plus the request-plus-24-hour cap (cancellation wins any race and marks the new row due in the same transaction), idempotent keyed failures that attach and queue any known Meta id, per-job recovery where unknown-id claims resolve only on a definitive provider no-match after the guard, expired-preview terminalization before artifact cleanup, a database-derived public-upload block at 3 unresolved failures, and the bounded operator delete-request queue; every state change is one transaction and every crash boundary recovers through `list_recoverable`/`recover_job`.
- Why we built it: Lifecycle intent must survive worker restarts and mid-call crashes without a second upload, a lost delete, or process-local state the other worker cannot see.

## How this was built

- Why we built it: Makes release evidence readable alongside the product.

## Image match gate

The offline gate registers an edit against the source background and reports
SAM re-find IoU, masked colour SSIM and an edit/no-op floor. Missing or
ambiguous masks, failed registration and uncalibrated SSIM refuse acceptance.
The local catch table consumes saved re-find masks and makes no model calls.
Fixed blur, recolour and shift recipes alter only the source part mask.

The ten Muse Image edits of the pre-event clip were made offline on 26 Oct 2026; the gate was run on them on 29 Oct 2026. The edit check is not part of the live app.

The hosted SAM adapter currently derives masks from returned boxes; IoU is
therefore box-mask IoU, not evidence of pixel-accurate segmentation.

## Long footage in 60-second chunks
- What it does: Accepts videos up to a configured maximum (300 s) instead of refusing over 60 s: the upload route, the preview transcode, and the served page copy all read the duration cap from config. Past one chunk the pipeline cuts the clip into configured 60 s windows with a 2 s overlap (local ffmpeg stream copy), runs the unchanged Spark step extraction per chunk with each chunk reserving and settling its own ledger share under its own admitted sub-job (the per-run 60 s envelope is untouched), then stitches the chunk bundles into one guide on the original timeline (times offset, a step spanning a boundary merged, overlap sightings deduped, rival windows kept and flagged). SAM, read-back, and rendering run as before on the stitched guide; when a stitched guide holds more SAM slots than the per-job budget fits, finished slots are kept and the rest stay unchecked instead of failing the job. The job's total is the per-chunk share times the chunk count, refused up front when it exceeds the configured job cap ($1.60, grown from the $0.50 single-job envelope to admit six chunks; the per-job ledger caps are unchanged and every chunk enforces them separately). Any budget refusal during extraction — up-front, reserve, or settle — now reports "step extraction over the budget limit" instead of "step extraction failed". Replay mode is unchanged: short clips take the exact single-chunk path with no sub-jobs.
- Why we built it: A full 3:34 teardown clip was refused by design (a 60 s upload cap plus a per-run token reservation sized for 60 s), so longer teardowns could never produce a guide.

## Per-chunk evidence with retry/resume of the failing chunk
- What it does: Every paid Spark raw is persisted immediately after its call succeeds, so a StepsDisagreement still leaves all three raws for offline diagnosis; each finished chunk's consensus bundle is persisted atomically under a parent-scoped digest (parent job, source video hash, chunk window, prompt/schema/model, span mode, consensus config, and the cut's real start and decoded duration — never an ephemeral file or child job id), and a re-entry after interruption re-cuts and probes each chunk locally (free) to validate the resume, reusing finished bundles with zero upload or Spark calls for those chunks; only a record whose cut still matches the bytes just probed is reused. One chunk's disagreement is retried as a new internal child attempt with its own ledger cap and idempotency key (finished chunks are never rerun); each retry is preflighted against the per-video retry budget and the job cap and refused up front with an honest message when it would cross either. Corrupt, TTL-expired, pre-cut-facts, or malformed-cut bundles fail closed and only that chunk reruns. Bundle records hold the consensus steps, a digest echo, the store time, and the cut's real start and decoded duration — no footage, frames, file ids, or credentials — under the configured data root's atomic stage-cache store. Retry spend is priced from each retry attempt's durable ledger reservations and actuals, never as retry-count times the current Spark price, and the per-chunk attempt cap is enforced against the durable next attempt number across restarts. One chunk's exclusive extraction claim spans its bundle lookup through its bundle save, and the bundle is rechecked after the claim and before any paid call, so two workers never pay Spark for the same chunk. Wild bundle timestamps fail closed to rerunning that chunk only; a failed child whose settlement stays pending stops the retry and reports pending settlement to the parent; each finished attempt's upload drains at attempt end and re-entries drain earlier runs' uploads. A worker that loses a chunk claim reports contention and leaves the shared parent active for the holder to finish, instead of failing it; a cancellation whose child settlement stays pending reports pending settlement to the parent; deeply nested corrupt bundle JSON is a cache miss for that chunk only.
- Why we built it: One chunk's disagreement used to lose the paid raw payloads and force a full rerun of the whole video.

## Waiting screen: named stages, real times, honest clock
- What it does: The job-status page (`/jobs/{id}`) shows five named stages from `config/wait.toml` (Upload your clip, Find the steps, Outline the parts, Check each outline, Bring the guide together), each marked Done, Working, In queue, Waiting or Stopped (a queued job shows the stages it has receipts for as done and the next one as in queue; a duration is left out when its start is unknown) from the job's own model-call receipts and stage marker, with how long each finished stage really took. A clock counts from the job's real start (labelled "Waiting for", "Running for" or "Ran for"), with no finish estimate and no fake percentage. "FRAME n / N outlined" counts the masks on disk against the picked frames. The last lines of the run log come from the receipts, shown in the viewer's local time. While the job runs the page refreshes its live parts in place (and still refreshes without script); it follows the redirect when the guide is ready, and a stopped job freezes its clock at its last activity. The stage list is validated by a strict loader (`app/wait_config.py`) and shipped to Modal with the image.
- Why we built it: A wait is easier to bear when the person can see what is happening; fake progress would mislead, so every number on the screen is read from the job's own records.

## Demo video tooling

## Model call receipts
- What it does: Records every model call (model, endpoint, latency, units, decimal cost, status, stage, bounded evidence) and a minimum event vocabulary over the ledger's tables, and renders `/calls/<job>` with observed run count, median, worst, per-call cost/status, and the natural sample read-back row first; when no read-back was recorded it says so and shows no fallback example.
- Why we built it: Anyone can inspect what the models did and what it cost without trusting a claim; missing evidence stays missing and private data (keys, headers, paths, IPs, consent text) is never stored or rendered.

## One correction control, numbered image zip, offline follower export
- What it does: On the guide page the person who made the guide (whoever holds its private link) can rename a part label or delete it, and that is the only editing; it lives in the filmed view's part rows, so the fixing (reader) view never shows it. Each change is a plain form post that appends to a small operation log beside the guide (`guide.json` is never rewritten) and every view replays the log, so the page, the image zip and the export all show the corrected guide; a rename keeps the label's pixels and checks, a delete shifts later parts down. It has tested failure states, each shown in plain words and changing nothing: blank or too-long name, a part that changed since the page loaded, an unusable name, a failed save, an edit cap reached, and an uneditable guide (the shared sample is one copy for every visitor, so it is never editable). The guide end links two downloads: a zip with one image per pictured step, showing every passed label where present and the original frame otherwise, and the follower export, one offline `guide.html` plus a local `images/` folder that opens in reader view with tools, difficulty, verdict, per-step label status and a functional hard stop that hides later steps until acknowledged (no script needed); the maker detail sits behind the view toggle. The export makes no network request, carries no tracking and calls no model: its build fails on any external reference, form, script source, `url()` or network call, and a browser test opens it offline and checks every image renders. Both zip files are built in memory on each request. The service stores no export copy to delete; the database download link expires after the configured 24 hours and its row is removed by the existing sweep. Published guides and correction sidecars remain until guide deletion; unpublished previews retain the existing 24-hour job expiry.
- Zip member times: both zips write every member with a fixed time from `config/exports.toml` (`[zip] date_time`) instead of file mtimes, so a source file with a 1970 mtime (the installed sample) no longer fails the download with a 500.
- Why we built it: One fix-before-share control, a numbered image set and an offline follower copy replace every other editor, so a viewer can see a repair guide corrected, taken away and followed with no connection.

## Extraction service retry
- What it does: When the AI service times out, has a transport or 5xx failure, returns an empty answer without token usage, or returns an answer that fails the step schema, one window call may be retried once after a short pause. The extra call has its own ledger reservation and receipt and starts only when the parent and window have enough time, the upload is not cancelled, and the shared retry and job budgets can cover it. A malformed answer is retried like any other service failure. If the service still does not answer, the status says the clip was fine and asks the user to try again; an earlier reason is preserved.
- Why we built it: A single failed call on a valid clip previously stopped the guide and incorrectly said no reliable repair steps were found.

## Aligned step consensus and content-first timing scorer
- What it does: Replaces exact text-plus-parts identity in Spark consensus and chunk stitching with one shared deterministic monotonic phase alignment (temporal overlap/order first, then the shared same-action predicate over one strict signature per step — in contiguous/phases span mode every known verb anywhere in each conjunction clause with verb sets matching when equal or differing only by configured transition verbs, identical normalised wordings matching on shared part evidence with equal qualifier sets even with no known verb, and verb-less phases linking their mutual-best overlap partner past a configured overlap fraction; in legacy/sparse span mode each clause's leading verb only with strictly equal verb sets and noun-only wordings never matching even when identical; in both modes no negation, equal qualifier sets, a shared non-generic part, one-to-many segmentation splits supported, no transitive bridge across unlinked endpoints, majority of distinct runs required, honest disagreement kept and flagged with part evidence); the old exact-key grouping stays only as a configured `exact` rollback (`PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_MODE`). Adds the canonical timing scorer, which aligns guide to ground truth monotonically by the shared predicate under the contiguous rules, counts unmatched GT rows as missing and unmatched guide rows as extra, then applies the unchanged ±3 s start-and-end rule and reports coverage, boundary accuracy, and the strict 7/9 pass separately. The generic-noun list, verb synonym groups, negation words, qualifiers, part synonym groups, transition verbs, and verb-less overlap fraction are configured (`PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_GENERIC_TOKENS`, `PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_VERB_SYNONYMS`, `PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_NEGATION_WORDS`, `PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_QUALIFIERS`, `PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_PART_SYNONYMS`, `PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_TRANSITION_VERBS`, `PARTFIDELITY_STEP_ALIGNMENT_VERBLESS_OVERLAP_FRACTION`). Ships a 12-payload replay fixture whose exact-mode replay reproduces a live six-step guide byte-for-byte.
- Why we built it: A live run scored 0/9: exact-key consensus erased three fully-observed phases (intro, cover pry-off, internal-battery removal) over wording/part-tuple differences, and ordinal timing pairing then reported the one timing hit as a miss. Alignment restores coverage while staying conservative (offline replay: 0/9 matched vs 0/9, strict 0/9 — qualifier-asymmetric and generic-only pairs fail closed as honest missing rows instead of hiding wrong guides). That replayed guide scores 0/9 strict because its one former pass paired external-battery removal with the internal-battery GT row, which qualifier rules now correctly reject.

## Extraction that survives half-answers
- What it does: Each of the three Spark runs gets its own single retry (the retry child has its own claim, budget and deadline checks), and a billed 200 whose step text is unusable counts as a retryable service failure, so a clip the provider half-answers can still finish. When exactly one run still fails after its retry and two valid runs remain, the guide is built from those two (majority of two, consensus_min_runs=2, fails loud if misconfigured); the disagreement gate is unchanged, the stored bundle records two source runs, and the guide carries a DRAFT note that fewer checks ran (never worded as verified, safe or accurate). With fewer than two valid runs the job fails as before. Every job that fails for any cause other than the runs disagreeing shows one reason, "The AI service did not answer properly for this video. Try again."; "no reliable steps" appears only when runs succeeded and disagreed. Fuse-lite (fuse_lite=true) folds a short repeat of an earlier action, and title-card steps, into the step before it, keeping tools, parts and hazards; it changes nothing on the saved H1, V3 and V4 guides.
- Why we built it: Owner clips V3 and V2 failed on one empty or unusable provider answer, with a different reason each time, although two of three runs would have served.

## Whole-video extraction with clock, cuts, snap, fold, and 12 steps
- What it does: The step cap moves from 8 to 12 end to end as the one `MAX_STEPS` value (strict schema, prompt, validation, ack links, consensus), since a live 11-step run proved 8 refuses real repairs. Videos within `PARTFIDELITY_WHOLE_VIDEO_MAX_S` (default 240 s) extract as ONE window through the chunk path with one chunk — but only when the honest window-sized reservation (per-second input-token bound plus the output cap, times the run count) fits the configured Spark reservation at the active prices (`PARTFIDELITY_SPARK_RESERVATION_USD`, default $0.48675 for 3 runs of a 240 s clip plus a 10% overrun margin); otherwise planning falls back to 60 s chunks, so a planned single window never refuses at client build time. Each window's Spark client is sized from the probed decoded duration rather than the nominal window and built before upload, so an unfundable window refuses before any bytes move. Every extraction chunk is clocked in place before upload (large m:ss.s overlay drawn with OpenCV and piped to ffmpeg/libx264, since this ffmpeg has no drawtext; window-relative times; `PARTFIDELITY_CLOCK_BURN_IN`, default on) and measured for scene-cut candidates (`select='gt(scene,T)'`, `PARTFIDELITY_SCENE_CUT_THRESHOLD` default 0.15, stored per window beside the job). The prompt may list the cuts with the validated guidance plus the change-of-action definition (`PARTFIDELITY_SCENE_CUTS_IN_PROMPT`, default on by the replay result: one run 7/9, consensus 6/9). After consensus in contiguous mode, each internal boundary snaps to its nearest cut within `PARTFIDELITY_SNAP_WINDOW_S` (default 2 s, sequentially, never crossing a neighbour), phases shorter than `PARTFIDELITY_MIN_PHASE_LENGTH_S` (default 5 s) fold into the neighbour they agree with (else the shorter one; the fold enforces the minimum deterministically, so the prompt does not repeat it), and every boundary keeps its run support, cut evidence, snap record, and check flag on the bundle for later review. The 60 s-and-under direct path is byte-identical (no clock, no cuts, no refine), and legacy span mode skips the refine pass exactly as before. One shared window estimate (run count times the per-run worst case for the window's input-token bound plus the output cap) prices admission, preflight, client holds, retry accounting, and child selection alike: direct and no-window jobs keep the $0.26 default cap, explicit windows install the window-sized cap only when the estimate fits the configured reservation, and a config whose read-back worst case exceeds the $0.04 per-call hold refuses before any job.
- Why we built it: Extraction must stop losing to chunk seams and clock-less guesses: the whole-video probe showed a 214 s / 57 MB clip extracting in one call in ~30 s, and its clock variant placing 17/37/60/87 within 1 s where the plain variant placed none. The follow-up probe then showed the cut list in the prompt reaching 7/9 in one run with every boundary landing on a listed cut.

## Failed jobs always say why; long clips and empty answers no longer fail
- What it does: Three causes of the live "This guide stopped" with no reason are fixed. (1) The failure reason marker was published with a hard link, which the Modal volume refuses (PermissionError, logged only), so `/jobs` showed `reason: null`; it now uses an exclusive create (first reason wins), and `/jobs` and the stopped page fall back to the generic failure copy so a failed job never has a null reason. (2) A 200 reply that billed tokens but held no step text was treated as a hard client error; it now counts as an empty answer, so the one configured extract retry runs and a second miss shows the "AI service did not answer" message. (3) On clips cut into 60 s chunks, neighbouring cuts overlap by the chunk overlap plus cut slop (2.134 s on the 279 s clip), just over the 2 s seam tolerance, so the final cover check failed every long clip; a step pair that crosses a real chunk seam is trimmed to the next step's start by that seam's own overlap before the check, while any other overlap still fails.
- Why we built it: Two of three owner clips failed on the live site with no explanation. The V2 failure was replayed offline from the saved chunk bundles of both attempts (both now pass); V3's cause is read from the app log.

## Home page scroll film
- What it does: The home page opens on a pinned scroll film. Our Blender render of the laptop teardown (250 frames) is drawn on a canvas and picked by scroll progress with eased smoothing, while three chapters (the screen, the keyboard, inside) follow the teardown stages: one caption at a time, switched on the exact frame the stage's first part starts to rise, with no frame between the opening copy and the last frame that lacks readable copy, so the lid closing and the closed laptop keep their section label. On wide screens the caption sits in a column on the left, vertically centred and clear of the model; on phones it sits below the stage. The film scroll is 380 vh. Each part is labelled once it has landed (and the label goes when that part starts back in), on a thin leader line from the part to its name in a column beside the stack (all on the right on wide screens, right and left in turn on phones, wrapping rather than running off the screen). The labels follow the parts frame by frame from `anchors.json`: the render writes where each part's outermost points fall in every frame (`tools/teardown_render.py --mode anchors`), the build keeps the labelled parts, and the page fetches it once the first bundle is in. A missing or malformed file is retried (`load_retries` times, waiting `load_backoff_ms` x 2^try); a bundle that fails is retried the same way, and if either stays unavailable the page goes back to the readable static layout (poster and chapters as blocks). The part names are also a real list (`#film-parts`, from `[[parts]]`, hidden visually) tied to the film art by `aria-describedby`, so screen readers and no-script readers get them while the moving labels stay decoration. Names come from `config/film.toml` `[[parts]]`. The loader refuses chapters that leave a gap, overlap or start off their stage. Phones and narrow screens load the 900 px frames, wide screens the 1600 px set, chosen once by viewport width and pixel ratio; the stage composition (phone or wide) follows the same configured css-width breakpoints. Loading is progressive: the poster first, then every 8th frame, every 4th, then all, and the nearest loaded frame is always drawn, so a fast scroll never shows a blank. The frames arrive in seven bundle files per set instead of 250 requests: each bundle holds one pass's frames in load order, is read as a stream so a frame shows as soon as its bytes are in, and its frames are decoded off the main thread; decoded bitmaps are held only for a window of frames around the scroll position (`bitmap_window`), at most `max_pending_decodes` decodes run at once and none starts for a position the scroll has left, so the canvas never decodes a frame while scrolling and memory stays bounded. Each bundle record is checked (frame in range, length within `max_frame_bytes`, a RIFF/WEBP of exactly that length, no truncated tail) and a bad one fails that bundle. At 1 s added latency per request the full set went from 46 s to 5.1 s and the coarse pass from 9 s to 3 s, and at 4x CPU the desktop scroll went from 47 dropped frames to none. Reduced motion, Save-Data and no JavaScript load no frames and get the poster with the chapters as static blocks. Every tunable (frame count, sets and breakpoints, passes, film height, chapter ranges, labels and copy, part labels) is in `config/film.toml`, read by the strict `app/film_config.py`. Frames ship as two plain tars in `assets/film/`; `tools/film_frames.py unpack` (make film-frames; the Modal image build and the test session run it) packs them into the bundle files in the served folders (records of frame number, length and the unchanged webp bytes; any other file there is removed), where they get a one-year cache header under a versioned URL; a test proves every bundle the page fetches is in the shipped image. The display is one solid dark module: a cover glass with a lit edge bonded to one panel (a dark cell over a bevelled gunmetal backlight unit with its driver board), with a dark satin back (`reflector`, `reflector_rough`, `reflector_spec` in `config/teardown.toml`), so no stack of pale sheets shows anywhere in the film. The render camera eases from the top view to one wide shot (85 mm, about 2 m away) while the lid shuts at frame 40 and then never changes, so the laptop stays one size on screen from the closed laptop to the exploded hold, with no counter-zoom keys and no CSS scaling of the 1600 px frames. The film is 250 frames at 380 vh: the parts rise 8 frames apart from frame 44, finish rising at 128, hold until 150, go back lowest first from 150 to 200 with their screws, and the complete laptop with "Keep the context" holds for the last 50 frames, about 56 vh of pinned scroll (280 vh of travel over 249 frames). Chapters 2 and 3 start at the keyboard (frame 60) and the logic board (frame 84). Part labels go as their part starts back in (`leave` in `config/film.toml` `[[parts]]`, checked against `config/teardown.toml` `[reassemble]` by a test), and the "Keep the context" caption stays to the last frame. The upload limit on the page reads the settings value ("up to 5 minutes").
- Why we built it: The line-art laptop was a stand-in; the home page needs a real rendered film that works on phone and desktop, keeps the explanatory copy readable throughout, and stays fast on slow networks. Loading one request per frame took about 46 s from India to the us-east host, so the scroll stayed choppy for most of a first visit.

## Modal deploy with snapshot-and-reconcile persistence
- What it does: Configures one Modal container with the live SQLite database on local disk. The SQLite backup API produces a checked snapshot on the Volume, which is fsynced, renamed and committed; the previous good snapshot is retained. Startup restores the newest usable snapshot and reconciles before serving, then requires a successful snapshot and Volume commit. A missing live artifact is tombstoned and its job expired; unreferenced job files are removed, while artifact and canonical guide references are retained. An inaccessible snapshot is tried alongside the previous one, and startup fails if neither is usable and either was inaccessible. The in-process sweep handles retention. The pending-settlement marker and sample pointer use fsynced sibling files and atomic replacement.
- Why we built it: SQLite is not run live on the Volume. Work since the last successful snapshot may be lost on restart. The health endpoint returns 503 when snapshot freshness exceeds `snapshot_max_age_s`, allowing the sample monitor to detect persistent commit failure; it does not refuse writes or enforce a hard loss bound. Reconciliation checks file existence, not file contents, and other job-file writers do not all provide an fsync guarantee. Actual Volume crash and restart behavior requires a real Modal test.

## Open-weights second check of part labels
- What it does: After the primary read-back, a background thread asks Meta's open-weights Spark Open (on Baseten, low reasoning effort, 640 px JPEG, strict yes/no that fails closed) whether each kept fan or battery label is really visible. It never delays guide finish, confirm or the user, and never changes a label. Each part ends as one `spark-open` receipt on `/calls/<job>` with latency and cost, and one line "Second check (Meta open weights, Spark Open): agrees / disagrees / not available"; a disagreement tells the person to check that label themselves. The per-job call and cost caps are one budget for the whole job, shared by the first batch and every later recheck, and kept in the job's corrections file so they hold across restarts and workers. A timeout, error, garbled answer, missing `BASETEN_API_KEY`, or a reached per-job call or cost cap shows "not available" and nothing else changes. Every tunable (on/off, model, endpoint, effort, image size, timeout, caps, concurrency, prices) lives in `config/open_readback.toml`, read by a strict loader; with `enabled = false` behaviour is identical to before. Spend is bounded before sending: each call reserves its worst case (prompt template plus a label of at most `label_max_chars`, one image of at most `image_max_px` counted at `image_token_ceiling` tokens, and `max_tokens` out) at the configured prices, a config whose per-job cap cannot cover one such call fails app startup, a call sent with no usage back is charged that whole reservation (marked `estimate`), and a call never sent costs $0 (marked `skipped`). One attempt per call, no retries. Deleting a job stops its checks before cancellation commits and never waits: queued ones are dropped and an in-flight one sends nothing more, so no send begins after the cancellation point. While a call is still in flight the job is already hidden and shows as pending, download links are revoked, and its local files stay queued; the sweeper waits for the check to finish (up to `cancel_wait_s`) and deletes them only then, retrying on its next pass otherwise. The workers stop on app shutdown without holding it up. `BASETEN_API_KEY` is provisioned in the deploy's named Secret (`deploy/modal_deploy.toml`).
- Why we built it: On a small probe the open model answered in the expected strict yes/no format within the latency budget at about $0.001 a call, but with 3.9 to 32 s latency, too slow for the user's path and fine as a quiet second opinion on the hosted read-back, which stays the primary check.

## Time-overlap timing scorer
- What it does: The canonical timing scorer pairs each ground-truth step one-to-one with the guide step that maximises total time overlap (an exact Hungarian assignment, never greedy; only positive-overlap pairs count, so unmatched truth rows report as missing and unmatched guide rows as extra). Wording never vetoes a pair: each pair reports the shared `same_action` verdict under the contiguous rules plus a token Jaccard similarity, without changing the pass count. The pass rule is unchanged (both |d_start| and |d_end| within ±3 s; the run passes at the 7-of-9 ratio by exact integer arithmetic), and strict passes at ±2 s and ±1 s are reported alongside, never as the bar. The tolerance, the strict lines, and the pass line live in `app.contracts.TIMING_SCORE_LAW`, read through a loader that fails loudly on a missing key.
- Why we built it: The scorer measured wording, not boundaries: a reference ground-truth list scored against itself only 6/9 (missing 1, 6, 7) because the wording gate vetoed time-exact pairs ("battery" is a generic token, qualifier sets must be equal), and a real Spark guide still scored 0-1/4 on chunk 0. The pass line was unreachable; a ground-truth list scored against itself now passes N/N on all four reference lists.

## Spark step extraction and confidence read-back
- What it does: Runs standard Spark three times per confirmed video, validates the strict step schema, aligns equivalent steps by timestamp plus action/parts similarity with a two-run majority (deeper disagreement fails closed instead of pretending consensus), and keeps median start/end times; steps whose runs disagree by more than 5 s get a "check time" badge with a preview frame and ±5 s nudges, and every step card gets "set start here" from the paused video. Each raw run and the consensus live in a replayable stage cache keyed by job, input, prompt, schema and model, and cached sample replay is permanently labelled with zero model calls. Labels below the frozen 0.70 confidence cutoff get at most three read-backs per job against the guide module's provisional render; a "no" immediately removes the label and tombstones its image, and a tombstone failure fails the job so cleanup still collects it.
- Why we built it: Turns one silent video into inspectable steps with honest approximate times, and removes doubtful labels instead of showing them.

## Time consensus for contiguous phases
- What it does: In contiguous phases mode, Spark consensus matches runs by time instead of wording: first every run must be an ordered, contiguous, non-overlapping partition (each phase end equals the next phase start exactly; any nonzero gap or overlap excludes the run); an invalid run is excluded, never normalized, and consensus fails closed when fewer than a majority of runs remain usable. A run missing the known chunk end beyond the contiguity tolerance — overshooting, short, or late-starting — is excluded the same way, and the prompt states the cut's probed decoded duration with every time bounded to `[0, duration]`, so one over-long run cannot stretch the tail and fragment runs gain no fabricated coverage. When the tolerance covers the whole clip (short last chunks) slack is meaningless, so a run must cover both endpoints literally or it is excluded. Each usable run's internal phase-boundary times cluster across runs within the boundary tolerance (`PARTFIDELITY_CONSENSUS_BOUNDARY_TOLERANCE_S`, defaulting to the run's own time-spread flag line) with at most one boundary per run per cluster, a cluster survives with support from a majority of runs at its median time (one-run-only boundaries drop), and the kept boundaries tile the known chunk extent `[0, duration]` with no gaps by construction, stitched back onto the source with the cut's real start. Each segment's label comes from the run phase with the largest time overlap, preferring the wording most runs agree on via complete-link grouping over the existing `same_action` predicate (no bridge merges candidates that disagree directly); wording disagreement only flags the kept segment (`time_needs_check`), never drops time. Legacy/sparse mode and `exact` mode keep the wording-grouped median consensus exactly as before, with the duration-less prompt and pre-extent digest shape, and the consensus digests key the boundary tolerance and the chunk extent so cached consensuses invalidate.
- Why we built it: Live Spark runs died in chunk 0 with `ContiguityError`: all three runs described the clip well, but consensus kept a phase only when at least two runs agreed on its wording, and every wording fix exposed a new wording split (verb-less phases, then one extra verb like "show"/"open"). Matching by time keeps every segment the runs collectively cover. Runs that overshot the 60 s chunk also stretched the observed-span consensus past the window, so the extent is now known, not observed.

## Same-action neighbour merge
- What it does: After snap and fold in contiguous phases mode (and on the direct short-video path, before ends derive), adjacent phases whose actions match after case, whitespace, and punctuation normalisation fuse into one phase spanning both, but only when that normalised key is non-empty (repeating to a fixpoint); the fused phase keeps the first phase's action, unions tools, parts, and hazards in order with duplicates dropped (parts capped to the 3-part contract limit), and takes the widest spread and any flag, with a shared kind when both agree else repair, while the edge between them and its record drop, surviving boundaries keep their records, and steps renumber from 1 with tiling preserved. Gated by `PARTFIDELITY_MERGE_SAME_ACTION` (default on) and bound into the chunk-bundle digest; legacy and exact modes skip the refine pass exactly as before.
- Why we built it: A live whole-video run split one verify phase in two (170.43-179.1 and 179.1-189.23, both "Power on and verify in OS"), and the overlap pairing then failed a step on the split instead of the boundary. An offline replay fuses the pair and scores 8/9.
